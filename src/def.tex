\mybox{Fundamental Theorem of Algebra}{
Every non-zero, single-variable, degree $n$ polynomial with complex coefficients has, counted with multiplicity, exactly $n$ complex roots.
\solidrule
\textbf{Reference:} \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_theorem_of_algebra}
}

\mybox{Semidefinite Programming (SDP)}{
Semidefinite programming (SDP) is a special case of convex optimization defined as:
\begin{mini*}
{\X \in \symm^n}{ \langle \C, \X \rangle}{}{}
\addConstraint{\langle \A_k, \X \rangle}{\leq b_k \qquad k = 1, \dots, m}
\addConstraint{\X}{\succeq 0}
\end{mini*}
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\C, \A_k \in \symm^n$ and $b_k \in \reals$ are given
\item $\X \in \symm^n$ is the optimization variable
\item $\symm^n$ is the set of $n \times n$ symmetric matrices
\item $\X \succeq 0$ means that $\X$ is positive semidefinite (i.e., all eigenvalues of $\X$ are non-negative)
\item $\langle \A, \B \rangle$ of two arbitrary matrices $\A, \B$ is the Frobenius inner product defined as:
$$\langle \A, \B \rangle = \A \bullet \B = \Tr(\A^\top \B) = \sum_{i,j} A_{ij} B_{ij}$$
\end{itemize}

\solidrule
\textbf{Reference:} \href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semidefinite_programming\#Equivalent_formulations}{Link to Wikipedia Article on Semidefinite Programming}
}
